Chess Piece Detection from Angled Photos

A three-person UC Davis project testing whether an oriented object detector can recognize chess pieces beyond ideal top-down views.

ECS 174 · Sep–Dec 2025 · Three-person team

Problem

Many chess-recognition systems assume a clear top-down camera angle and even lighting. Real photos introduce perspective distortion, glare, shadows, different piece styles, and overlapping pieces.

We asked whether YOLOv8 with an oriented bounding-box head could detect and classify pieces under these less controlled conditions.

Setup

Model
Pretrained YOLOv8n-OBB, fine-tuned to detect black and white chess pieces
Dataset
Kaggle chess dataset with real and synthetic images
Training
606 images · 640 px · 20 epochs · batch size 20
Validation
58 images · 386 labeled piece instances

Failure analysis

  • Pieces cut off at the image border were sometimes missed.
  • Small pawns were harder to separate from the background.
  • Low-contrast piece-and-square combinations reduced visual separation.
  • Heavy perspective overlap made occluded pieces harder to detect.

Limits and next step

The study used a modest validation split and did not include an external test set or an axis-aligned-box baseline. It detects pieces, but it does not yet reconstruct a legal chess position.

Next steps include perspective correction, board and FEN reconstruction, targeted augmentation for glare and extreme viewpoints, and real-world mobile testing.

My contribution

I collected chess images from online sources, trained the detection workflow, and reviewed lighting, occlusion, and cropped-piece failures. I documented the findings and proposed perspective correction and board-layout reconstruction as next steps.

Stack

Python, PyTorch, Ultralytics YOLOv8n-OBB, Google Colab, oriented bounding boxes, data augmentation, and object-detection evaluation.
